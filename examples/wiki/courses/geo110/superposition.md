# Superposition

In an undisturbed stack of sedimentary layers, each layer is younger than the
one below it (GEO 110 lecture 3). Two companion rules:

- **Original horizontality**: sediment is laid down flat, so tilted or folded
  layers were deformed after they formed.
- **Cross-cutting relationships**: a fault or intrusion is younger than every
  layer it cuts.

These give **relative** ages (older or younger), not ages in years. Numerical
ages need radiometric dating.

**Trap.** Superposition assumes the stack is undisturbed. Overturned folds put
older rock on top.
