# Rock Cycle

Rocks come in three families (GEO 110 lecture 1):

- **Igneous** rock forms when molten rock cools. Slow cooling underground gives
  large crystals (granite); fast cooling at the surface gives small ones (basalt).
- **Sedimentary** rock forms when weathered grains are deposited, compacted and
  cemented (sandstone, shale), or precipitate from water (limestone).
- **Metamorphic** rock forms when existing rock is changed by heat and pressure
  without melting (shale → slate, limestone → marble).

Any family can become any other: melting leads to igneous rock, weathering and
deposition to sedimentary, heat and pressure to metamorphic.

**Trap.** Metamorphism happens in the solid state. If the rock melts, what forms
on cooling is igneous.
